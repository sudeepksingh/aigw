Demo data. A simulated fleet: these homes, problems and rollouts are made up (python3 -m aigw_cortex demo, or the AIGW OS demo image). Nothing here is a real home.

Fleet health

Homes to call first

An open problem, a warning that a fault is near, a poor score, or a gateway gone quiet.

Before the call

Faults a customer would notice: fixed by AIGW itself (as its act mode allows), or raised to the ISP with a note for the customer in plain words.

Mesh Wi-Fi

Homes whose extenders hurt more than they help: a weak or slow backhaul with devices behind it. Moving the extender, a cable, or a better extender fixes it; AIGW moves devices that hear the gateway well enough off it meanwhile.

Wi-Fi coverage

Homes that need an extender: a weak spot that two or more devices share, so the gateway is badly placed for it. Homes where one device is weak in a far room only need that device moved: no sale.

ISP tickets

Area outages and proactive tickets in the ISP's own ticketing system (TMF621 or signed webhooks), and whether each one got there.

Staged rollouts

A rollout stops by itself when a wave gets worse. Pause or abort here; resuming is done at the command line (rollout resume).

ISP signals

What homes would buy, or why they may leave, as each gateway saw it.
